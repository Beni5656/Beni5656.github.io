Feature Matching for Autostitching

Part A needed hand-clicked correspondences. Here the points are found automatically, so a pair of photos goes straight to a mosaic. The photos are new ones where I only turned the camera in place, because the descriptors below are not rotation invariant. The steps are shown on the library pair, and the mosaics for all three pairs are at the end.

B.1Harris corners and ANMS

I used the Harris corner response with the harmonic mean, det / trace, and kept the local maxima in a 5×5 window that pass a threshold, skipping a border near the edge. That gave 5062 corners for the first library image, which is too many and bunches up on textured areas like the grass. Adaptive non-maximal suppression fixes that. Each corner gets a radius, the distance to the nearest corner that is clearly stronger (with a robustness factor of 0.9), and I kept the 500 corners with the largest radius.

B.2Feature descriptors

For each corner I took a 40×40 window from a blurred copy of the image and sampled every 5th pixel to get an 8×8 patch. Each patch is normalized to zero mean and unit variance, so the descriptor doesn't care about brightness or contrast changes between the two shots. B.3Matching

Each descriptor is compared against all the descriptors in the other image. I only keep a match if the nearest neighbor is closer than 0.7 times the distance to the second nearest, which throws out ambiguous features such as the repeating windows. 166 matches survived for the library pair. Green lines below are the ones RANSAC later keeps and red lines are the ones it rejects.

B.4RANSAC and mosaics

A few bad matches still get through the ratio test and they would wreck a least squares fit. So RANSAC picks 4 random matches, computes a homography, and counts how many of the other matches land within 4 pixels of where they should. After 5000 tries I keep the largest set of inliers and recompute the homography from all of them. The mosaics then use the same warping and blending as Part A.
