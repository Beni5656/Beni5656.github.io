Part A: The Power of Flow Models

Part 1.1Constructing noisy training inputs

I used a photo of a church facade, cropped to 512×512, and noised it with xt = (1 − t)ε + t x1, using the same noise sample at every time.

Part 1.2Classical denoising

I divided xt by t and applied a Gaussian blur, keeping the σ that came closest to the original.

Part 1.3One-step denoising

One-step estimate x̂1 = xt + (1 − t)vθ(xt, t, c) with the prompt "a high quality photo".

Part 1.4Visualizing velocity

Predicted update (1 − t)v̂t, ground truth update (1 − t)vgt with vgt = x1 − ε, and the per-pixel RGB RMS error between them. Both updates use the same symmetric color scale with zero at gray, and the error maps use the same limits at all three times.

The predicted update gets the overall structure right at every time, like the doorway, columns, and steps. What it misses is fine detail. The error shows up along edges, the statues, and the door panels, which is the stuff the noise wiped out. At t = 0.25 the prediction is noticeably smoother than the ground truth, and by t = 0.75 the two are almost the same and the error map is mostly dark.

Part 1.5Iterative denoising with Euler sampling

Euler sampling with h = 0.02, starting from the t = 0.50 noisy image (25 updates). I tested the sampler on a constant velocity field, including a start time where the last step gets shortened, and it ended exactly at t = 1 with the expected values each time.

The Gaussian blur is by far the worst, it's still noisy and all the detail is gone. The one-step result gets the building back but looks smooth and a little soft. The Euler result looks the most like a real photo, with sharp stone texture, statues, and steps. It doesn't match the original exactly though, since some details like the statues are made up by the model instead of recovered.

Part 1.6Generating images from noise

Every sample is a portrait of a person on a plain background, so the generic prompt clearly favors people. The quality is hit or miss though. Seeds 182 and 184 look real, but 180 has no face and 181 and 183 have smeared, distorted skin.

Part 2.1Classifier-free guidance

vcfg = vc + w(vc − vu) with w = 7 and "" as the unconditional prompt. Setting w = 0 gives back the conditional prediction. Same starting noise as 1.6.

With guidance every face comes out clean and sharp, including the three that were broken before, and the contrast is much higher. Each one still loosely matches its unguided version (seed 182 keeps the hand by the face). The downside is that they look more alike, with similar poses and plain dark or light backgrounds.

Part B: Flow Matching from Scratch

Part 1.2Noising process

z = x + σε on MNIST test digits. Each row is one σ, from top to bottom: 0.0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.8, 1.0.

Part 1.2.1Training a denoiser

UNet with D = 128 trained to denoise at σ = 0.5 for 5 epochs, batch size 256, Adam with learning rate 1e-4, with fresh noise every batch.

Part 1.2.2Out-of-distribution testing

The σ = 0.5 denoiser on the same test digit at σ = 0.0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.8, 1.0 (left to right). Top row is the noisy input, bottom row is the output.

Part 1.2.3Denoising pure noise

Same setup as 1.2.1, but the input is pure noise z = ε.

Every output is the same blurry blob, no matter what noise goes in. It doesn't look like any one digit. It looks like a bunch of digits stacked on top of each other: you can make out a 0 shaped outline with hints of a 3, 8, and 9 in the middle. After epoch 5 it's basically the same image, just smoother.

This happens because the input has no information about which digit the target is. The noise is independent of the image, so the best the model can do under MSE is output the one image that minimizes the average squared distance to all training digits, which is their mean (the centroid). That's why it looks like an average of all the classes 0 to 9, weighted by how often each stroke shows up, and why the loss flattens out early and stops improving.

Without the learning rate scheduler

To make up for removing the scheduler, I trained with a constant learning rate of 1e-3 instead of starting at 1e-2. Without the decay, 1e-2 would stay high for the whole run and make the end of training noisy, so I picked a smaller value close to where the scheduled run ends up by the last epoch. Everything else is the same.
